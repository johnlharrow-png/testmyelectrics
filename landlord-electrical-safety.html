Landlord electrical safety: the rules in England

If you rent out a home in England, the law says its wiring must be inspected and tested at least every 5 years. Here is what the Electrical Safety Standards Regulations 2020 ask of you, in plain English.

Your duties as a landlord

Under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector and Social Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020.

  1. Keep the electrical installation up to the standards in the wiring regulations (BS 7671) for as long as the home is let.
  2. Have it inspected and tested by a qualified person at least every 5 years, or sooner if the last report says so.
  3. Give each existing tenant a copy of the report within 28 days of the inspection.
  4. Give a new tenant a copy before they move in, and a prospective tenant a copy within 28 days if they ask in writing.
  5. Send the council a copy within 7 days if it asks in writing.
  6. Keep a copy until the next inspection, and give one to the electrician who does it.
  7. If the report says work is needed, have it done by a qualified person within 28 days, or sooner if the report says so.
  8. Get written confirmation that the work is done. Send it, with the report, to your tenants and the council within 28 days.

What has to be put right

C1 and C2

Danger present, or potentially dangerous. These make the report Unsatisfactory, and you must have them put right within 28 days, or sooner if the report says so.

C3

Improvement recommended. The government guidance says a C3 doesn't need remedial work. It's worth planning for, but the report can still be Satisfactory.

FI (further investigation). For inspections done before 30 November 2026, landlords must also have any FI item investigated within 28 days. An amendment to the regulations takes out the wording about further investigation for inspections from 30 November 2026.

HMOs and licensed properties

If you let a house in multiple occupation, the same EICR rules apply, and your licence may add its own conditions.

  • HMOs are covered

    The rules apply to homes shared by people from more than one household, such as house shares and bedsits.

  • HMO licences

    An HMO with 5 or more people from 2 or more households needs a licence from the council. Most councils we checked ask for a current EICR with the application.

  • Selective licensing

    Some councils also license ordinary rented homes in certain areas. Check with your council: our town pages say what we found for each area.

If the rules aren't followed

  1. The council can serve a remedial notice, giving you 28 days to do the work.
  2. It can fine you up to £40,000.
  3. You are not in breach if you can show you took all reasonable steps, for example if a tenant keeps refusing access. Keep a record of what you tried.

Wales and Scotland

Wales

Landlords must have a valid electrical condition report, renewed at least every 5 years, and give a copy to the contract-holder. This comes from the Renting Homes (Fitness for Human Habitation) (Wales) Regulations 2022.

Scotland

The Repairing Standard asks for an EICR at least every 5 years, with a copy for new tenants before the tenancy starts. Scotland also has its own alarm rules.

Landlord questions

How often does a rented home need an EICR?

At least every 5 years in England, or sooner if the last report says so. A new tenancy can start on the current report, as long as it is less than 5 years old and its next inspection date has not passed.

Do C3 items have to be fixed?

No. The government guidance says a C3 means an improvement is recommended, not that remedial work is required. Only C1 and C2 items make a report Unsatisfactory.

What if my tenant will not let the electrician in?

You are not in breach if you can show you took all reasonable steps to get the inspection done. Keep a record of your letters, emails and the dates you offered.

Do the rules cover lodgers?

No. Lodgers who share a kitchen, bathroom, toilet or living room with you or your family are excluded. So are leases of 7 years or more, student halls, hostels, refuges and care homes.

Do I need a PAT test as well?

These regulations cover the fixed wiring, not appliances. It is still sensible to have the appliances you supply PAT tested, and some councils ask for a PAT certificate when you apply for an HMO licence.

How much can the council fine me?

Up to £40,000 for breaching these regulations. The council can also serve a remedial notice giving you 28 days to put things right.
