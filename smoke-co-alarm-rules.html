Smoke and carbon monoxide alarm rules for landlords

Since 1 October 2022, landlords in England must have a smoke alarm on every storey of a rented home, and a carbon monoxide (CO) alarm in any room with a fixed combustion appliance. Here is what that means in practice.

Written by the NAPIT-registered electricians at testmyelectrics.com.

The four rules

Under the Smoke and Carbon Monoxide Alarm (England) Regulations 2015, as changed by the Smoke and Carbon Monoxide Alarm (Amendment) Regulations 2022.

  1. A smoke alarm on every storey that has a room used as living accommodation. Bathrooms and toilets count, and so do halls and landings.
  2. A CO alarm in every room with a fixed combustion appliance, if the room is used as living accommodation. Gas cookers are left out.
  3. Check every alarm works on the day a new tenancy starts. Keep a record, for example by having the tenant sign the inventory.
  4. Repair or replace any alarm you are told is faulty, as soon as reasonably practicable.

Which appliances need a CO alarm?

The guidance suggests fitting CO alarms at head height, on a wall or shelf, about 1 to 3 metres from the appliance, and following the maker's instructions.

  • Gas or oil boiler

    Any fixed appliance that burns fuel to make heat counts.

  • Wood or log burner

    Wood, coal and other solid fuel stoves count too.

  • Gas fire

    A fixed gas fire burns fuel to heat the room, so it needs one.

  • Gas cooker

    Gas cookers are the one exception. So is a decorative fireplace that isn't used.

Which alarms to fit

The law in England doesn't say which type to fit. These grades and levels come from the British Standard for alarms in homes, BS 5839-6.

  • Grade D1

    Mains powered, with a sealed standby battery. The usual choice for rented homes.

  • Grade D2

    Mains powered, with a standby battery you can replace.

  • Battery alarms

    Allowed in England. The guidance prefers sealed-for-life batteries to replaceable ones.

  • LD3 cover

    Smoke alarms in the hall and on each landing. The minimum for most existing homes.

  • LD2 cover

    LD3 plus the main living room, and a heat alarm in the kitchen. What we recommend for rented homes.

  • Replace at 10 years

    Alarms wear out. Check the replace-by date printed on each one.

If the rules aren't followed

  1. The council can serve a remedial notice, giving you 28 days to fit, repair or replace the alarms.
  2. If you don't, it can charge a penalty of up to £5,000.
  3. Licensed HMOs follow the same kind of rules through their licence conditions instead.

Alarm questions

Do I need a CO alarm for a gas cooker?

No. Gas cookers are the one fixed combustion appliance the rules leave out. You do need one in any room used as living accommodation with a boiler, gas fire, wood burner or other fixed appliance that burns fuel.

Can I fit battery smoke alarms?

Yes. The rules in England don't say what type of alarm to fit. The government guidance prefers alarms with sealed-for-life batteries to ones with replaceable batteries. Many landlords choose mains-powered alarms with a sealed standby battery.

Do the alarms have to be interlinked?

Not in England, though BS 5839-6 recommends it, so that every alarm sounds when one goes off. Wales and Scotland have their own alarm rules.

Do I need a heat alarm in the kitchen?

Not by law in England. A heat alarm in the kitchen is part of the higher LD2 level of protection in BS 5839-6, which we recommend for rented homes.

Who tests the alarms during the tenancy?

The rules only say you must check them on the first day of a new tenancy. After that, show your tenants how to test them and ask them to tell you about any fault. You must then repair or replace the alarm.

Do the rules apply to social housing?

Yes. Since 1 October 2022 they apply to homes let by private landlords and by social housing providers, unless the tenancy is excluded.
