What landlords and homeowners say

    EICR questions

    What is an EICR?

    An Electrical Installation Condition Report: a full inspection and test of your wiring, fuse board, sockets and fittings against the UK wiring regulations (BS 7671), with a certificate showing whether it's safe. What an EICR checks and what the codes mean.

    How often do landlords need one?

    At least every 5 years, under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector Regulations 2020. Give a copy to existing tenants within 28 days, to new tenants before they move in, and to the council within 7 days if they ask.

    How long does it take?

    Inspection times vary with the size of the property and how many circuits it has. The power will be off for short periods while circuits are tested, so it's worth saving any work and resetting clocks afterwards.

    When do I get the certificate?

    Within 48 hours, and only once your booking is paid in full. It's emailed to you and goes into your online account to download. If our engineer found more circuits than you booked, or we had to pay for parking, that final bill needs paying before the certificate is issued.

    What if something fails?

    We explain any C1 or C2 issues in plain English and quote for the work. Nothing is done without your approval. Landlords must complete remedial work within 28 days, or sooner if the report says so. C1, C2, C3 and FI explained.

    Can I change the date?

    Yes, free of charge, up to 24 hours before your appointment. See our booking terms.

    Do I need to be home?

    Not necessarily. Someone aged 18 or over needs to be there to let us in, or you can arrange access, for example with your tenant, your letting agent or a key safe. If no one answers when our engineer arrives, they'll wait 5 minutes and then leave, and the visit counts as missed. Before we arrive, please clear any furniture away from sockets and light switches, and unplug all electrical items.
