    FAQ

    EICR questions, straight answers.

    How often does a rental property need an EICR?

    In England, landlords must have the electrical installation inspected and tested at least every five years under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector Regulations 2020.

    You need to give a copy of the report to your tenants within 28 days, and to the local council within 7 days if they ask for it. Councils can fine landlords up to £30,000 for breaches.

    How long does an EICR take, and will the power be off?

    Times can vary, but an EICR takes no longer than 2–3 hours. The power will be off for short periods while circuits are tested, so it's worth saving any work and resetting clocks afterwards.

    When do I get my EICR certificate?

    We email it to you and save it in your online account, where you can download copies at any time. There's no password: we email you a login link.

    If there's nothing extra to pay, it's issued without any further payment. If our engineer found more circuits than you booked, or we had to pay for parking, it's issued as soon as that final bill is paid.

    What happens if my property fails?

    Any C1 (danger present), C2 (potentially dangerous) or FI (further investigation needed) code makes a report "unsatisfactory". C3 codes are recommendations and don't fail the report.

    We'll explain what we found and send a clear quote for any remedial work, which is only ever done with your approval. Landlords must complete remedial work within 28 days, or sooner if the report says so.

    What's an EICR, and do homeowners need one?

    An Electrical Installation Condition Report is a full inspection and test of your wiring, fuse board, sockets, lighting and earthing, checked against the UK wiring regulations (BS 7671).

    For owner-occupied homes, an EICR is recommended at least every 10 years, and it's a smart move before you buy a property or start a renovation.
