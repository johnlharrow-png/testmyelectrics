What is an EICR? The codes C1, C2, C3 and FI explained

An EICR (Electrical Installation Condition Report) is a full inspection and test of a property's wiring, fuse board, sockets and fittings. The electrician checks everything against the UK wiring regulations (BS 7671) and gives anything that isn't right a code that shows how serious it is.

What an EICR checks

The electrician looks over the whole fixed installation, then tests every circuit with calibrated instruments.

  • The fuse board

    Its condition, the circuit breakers and RCDs, and whether each circuit is labelled.

  • Earthing and bonding

    The main earth, and the earth connections to your gas and water pipes.

  • Sockets, switches and lights

    A sample is opened up to check the connections, polarity and signs of overheating.

  • Wiring

    Damage, wear and cables that are unsupported or in the wrong place.

  • Tests on every circuit

    Continuity, insulation resistance, polarity, earth fault loop impedance and RCD trip times.

  • Bathrooms and outdoors

    The extra protection needed where water and electricity meet.

Satisfactory or Unsatisfactory?

Satisfactory

No C1 or C2 codes. You can still have C3 recommendations and pass.

Unsatisfactory

At least one C1 or C2. Landlords must put these right within 28 days, or sooner if the report says so.

The codes on your report

Each problem the electrician finds is recorded once, at the most serious code that applies.

C1Danger present

Someone could get a shock or burn, or a fire could start, as things are today.

  • Live parts you can touch, such as a broken socket or a missing cover on the fuse board
  • Metalwork that has become live because of a fault
  • The live and neutral swapped over where the supply comes in

What happens next: We make it safe before we leave, with your agreement, usually by switching off and isolating that circuit. You get a written danger notice the same day.

C2Potentially dangerous

Not dangerous this minute, but one fault, knock or accident away from it.

  • A cable joint wrapped in tape instead of being in a proper enclosure
  • No earth bonding to the gas or water pipes
  • No RCD protection for sockets that could be used for equipment outdoors
  • An RCD that does not trip, or trips too slowly

What happens next: It needs putting right urgently. We send a plain-English, fixed quote and nothing is done without your OK.

C3Improvement recommended

Safe, but doesn't meet the current edition of the wiring regulations.

  • Circuits not labelled at the fuse board
  • No surge protection where it would now be fitted
  • An older type of RCD where a newer type is now needed, for example with an EV charger, solar panels or an induction hob
  • A plastic fuse board under the stairs or on the way out of the house

What happens next: Optional. A C3 on its own does not make the report Unsatisfactory.

FIFurther investigation

The electrician has found a reason to suspect a problem but couldn't get to the bottom of it during the inspection.

  • A circuit that can't be traced to where it ends
  • An RCD that keeps tripping with no obvious cause
  • Signs of damage to wiring hidden in walls or under floors

What happens next: It is a strong recommendation to get it looked into. For landlords it was a legal duty within 28 days for inspections before 30 November 2026; the rules dropped it for inspections from that date.

LIM (limitation) is not a fault. It lists anything the electrician couldn't get to or test, such as a boarded-over loft or a freezer that had to stay on, so the next electrician knows.

Things that don't fail an EICR on their own

Electrical Safety First's guidance for electricians says these should not be coded just because they are there.

  • Old-style rewireable fuses

    A fuse box with fuse wire isn't a fault in itself, as long as the fuses protect the circuits properly.

  • Old red and black wiring

    Cable colours changed in 2004. Older colours are fine if the wiring is in good condition.

  • No earth wire to the sink or bath

    Kitchen sinks don't need one, and most bathrooms don't when the circuits have RCD protection and the main bonding is in place.

  • No switches on sockets

    Unswitched sockets are still allowed. They don't need changing for an EICR.

For landlords: the rules in England

Under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020:

  1. Have an EICR done at least every 5 years, or sooner if the last report says so.
  2. Give your tenants a copy within 28 days of the inspection, and new tenants a copy before they move in.
  3. Send the council a copy within 7 days if they ask for one.
  4. Put right any C1 or C2 within 28 days, or sooner if the report says so. (For inspections before 30 November 2026, FI items also had to be investigated.)
  5. Give your tenants and the council written confirmation within 28 days of the work being finished.

EICR questions

How long does an EICR take?

It depends on the size of the property and how many circuits it has. A flat is usually a morning or an afternoon; a larger house can take most of the day. The power goes off for short spells while circuits are tested, so save any work and expect to reset clocks afterwards.

How often do I need one?

Landlords in England need one at least every 5 years, or sooner if the last report says so. For a home you live in, every 10 years is recommended, and whenever you buy or move into a property.

What happens if my report is Unsatisfactory?

We explain each C1 and C2 in plain English and send a fixed quote for the work. Nothing is done without your OK. Once it is done you get a certificate for the work, which goes with the report to show the installation is safe.

Do C3 recommendations have to be done?

No. A C3 means part of the installation does not meet the current edition of the wiring regulations but is not dangerous. It does not make the report Unsatisfactory. It is worth planning for, for example when you next have electrical work done.
